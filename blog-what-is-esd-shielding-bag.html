What Is an ESD Shielding Bag and When Do You Need It

How ESD shielding bags protect PCBs and semiconductors from static damage, and which type fits your handling and shipping process.

Electrostatic discharge (ESD) can silently damage PCBs, ICs and semiconductors — often with no visible sign. An ESD shielding bag is the standard way to protect sensitive parts in storage and transit. Here is what it is and when to use it.

1. What ESD Does to Electronics

A discharge of even a few hundred volts can degrade or destroy modern components (many ICs are sensitive below 100 V, per the Human Body Model). Damage can be catastrophic (immediate failure) or latent (a weakened part that fails later in the field). For any facility handling electronics, uncontrolled static is a direct yield and reliability risk.

2. How a Shielding Bag Works

An ESD shielding bag has a metalized layer that forms a Faraday cage, diverting external static fields around — not through — the contents. A moisture-barrier variant (MBB) adds a laminate that also blocks humidity, protecting moisture-sensitive (MSL) parts. The result is protection in storage and transit, not just on the bench.

3. Shielding vs Anti-Static vs Dissipative

TypeWhat it doesWhen to use
Anti-static (pink poly)Prevents charge building on the bag surfaceNon-critical items, inside an ESD-safe area
Static-dissipativeSlowly bleeds charge awayGeneral handling
Shielding (metalized)Blocks external E-fieldsTransit of sensitive components
Moisture-barrier (MBB)ESD plus humidity protectionShipping ICs / dies / MSL parts

4. Key Specifications and Standards

Specify to recognised standards: IEC 61340-5-1 and ANSI/ESD S11.4 (shielding effectiveness test requires the bag to limit a discharge to below 10 nJ inside), and MIL-PRF-81705 for moisture-barrier bags. Typical properties: surface resistance 10^6 to 10^11 ohms, and verified static-decay performance.

5. When You Need One

  • Moving bare PCBs, semiconductor dies or assembled boards outside an ESD-controlled area.
  • Inbound from suppliers, transfer between production lines, and outbound to customers.
  • Always pair with ESD-safe labels and tapes to keep the protection chain intact.

6. Verify and Handle Correctly

Use a shielding-bag tester to confirm integrity before reuse, keep bags sealed until use, and handle inside an ESD-protected area with wrist straps. A damaged or opened bag no longer provides full shielding.

7. How to Read Bag Markings

Shielding bags are marked with the international ESD symbol (a triangle with a striking hand) and often a moisture-sensitivity label for MBBs. Check the printed surface-resistance range and the shielding symbol before use; if the marking is missing or the bag is unverified, treat it as non-shielding.

FAQ

What is the difference between shielding and anti-static bags?

Anti-static bags only stop charge building on the bag; shielding bags actively block external static fields via a metalized layer — essential for transit of sensitive parts.

Can I reuse a shielding bag?

Only after testing it intact with a bag tester; once punctured or the seal is broken, shielding is compromised.

Do I need a moisture-barrier bag?

If the parts are moisture-sensitive (MSL-rated), yes — a moisture-barrier bag (MBB) adds humidity protection for shipping.

Which standard applies?

IEC 61340-5-1 is the widely used international ESD control standard; ANSI/ESD S11.4 covers shielding effectiveness testing.
