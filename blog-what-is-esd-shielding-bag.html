What Is an ESD Shielding Bag and When Do You Need It

How ESD shielding bags protect PCBs and semiconductors from static damage, and which type fits your handling and shipping process.

How ESD shielding bags protect PCBs and semiconductors from static damage, and which type fits your handling and shipping process.

What ESD Actually Does to Electronics

Electrostatic discharge (ESD) can silently damage or destroy sensitive components like PCBs, ICs and semiconductors — often with no visible signs. For any facility handling modern electronics, uncontrolled static is a direct yield and reliability risk.

How a Shielding Bag Works

An ESD shielding bag has a metalized layer that forms a Faraday cage, diverting static around the contents. A moisture-barrier variant adds a desiccant-friendly laminate to also block humidity. Together they protect parts in storage and transit, not just on the bench.

Shielding vs Anti-Static vs Dissipative

Anti-static bags only prevent charge build-up on the bag surface; dissipative bags slowly bleed charge; shielding bags actively block external fields. For unprotected sensitive components, only shielding (often with moisture barrier) gives real transit protection.

When You Need One

Use shielding bags whenever you move or store bare PCBs, semiconductor dies, or assembled boards outside an ESD-controlled area — inbound from suppliers, between lines, and outbound to customers. Pair with ESD-safe labels and tapes for a complete chain.
