Stretch Film Thickness Guide: Which Micron for Your Pallet

Choose the right stretch film gauge (17 to 30 micron) for hand vs machine wrap and keep pallet loads stable through transit.

Stretch film (stretch wrap) stabilises pallet loads for transit. Thickness — measured in microns (um) — is the single biggest lever on cost and load security. This guide helps you pick the right gauge for hand or machine wrapping.

1. Micron vs Gauge

Film is most often specified in microns. In some markets "gauge" is still used, where roughly 1 gauge is about 0.254 um (so 90 gauge is about 23 um). When comparing quotes, confirm the unit — a "90 gauge" and a "23 micron" film are the same thing.

2. Common Thicknesses and What They Mean

Thickness (um)Approx gaugeTypical use
12 to 15 um50 to 60Ultra-light; machine pre-stretch only
17 to 20 um67 to 80Light, uniform cartons; hand or machine
23 um90General-purpose pallets (the workhorse)
25 to 30 um100 to 120Heavy, sharp-edged or unstable loads
35 um+140+Very heavy loads / edge protection

3. Hand Wrap vs Machine Wrap

Hand wrap suits low-volume or irregular loads and favours comfortable, often pre-stretched grades. Machine wrap (on a pallet wrapper) delivers consistent containment force and uses thinner film efficiently through high pre-stretch. Choose grade by application, not just unit price.

4. Match Thickness to Load Weight and Stability

  • Light, uniform cartons: 17 to 20 um.
  • Standard pallets: 23 um.
  • Heavy, sharp-edged or unstable loads: 25 to 30 um, possibly with a reinforced edge band.
  • Under-wrapping causes load shift and damage; over-wrapping wastes film and money.

5. Pre-Stretch and Film Yield

Higher pre-stretch (for example 200 to 300 percent) means less film per pallet and lower cost — but do not exceed the film elongation limit or it will neck and tear. Track actual metres used per pallet to tune gauge and cut spend without risking load failure.

6. Film Type: Blown vs Cast

Blown (tubular) film offers higher puncture resistance and two-sided cling, good for heavy or irregular loads. Cast film is clearer, quieter to unwind and more uniform, preferred for clean, high-visibility pallets. Match the type to how the load is handled and presented.

FAQ

Which thickness for a standard pallet?

23 um (about 90 gauge) is the general-purpose workhorse for typical carton pallets.

Hand wrap or machine wrap?

Use hand wrap for low volume or irregular loads; machine wrap for consistent, higher-volume palletising.

Can a thinner film save money?

Yes — with machine pre-stretch you can drop to 17 to 20 um for light loads while keeping containment force.

How much film does one pallet need?

It scales with pallet height, wraps per revolution and overlap; measure usage per pallet to right-size the gauge.
