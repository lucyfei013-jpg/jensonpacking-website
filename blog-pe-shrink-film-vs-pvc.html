PE Shrink Film vs PVC Shrink Film

Why PE shrink film is replacing PVC for bottle multipacks and bundling, and how to choose the right material for clarity, safety and cost.

Shrink film unitizes bottles, cans and retail multipacks into stable bundles. For years PVC led on price and clarity, but PE has become the default for sustainability and compliance. Here is how the two compare and how to choose.

1. What Shrink Film Actually Does

Shrink film is applied loose and then passed through a heat tunnel, where it shrinks tightly around the product to bundle, protect and present it. The right film balances clarity, strength, shrink temperature and end-of-life handling.

2. PE vs PVC at a Glance

PropertyPE (Polyethylene)PVC (Polyvinyl chloride)
ClarityGoodVery high
Shrink temperatureLower (about 110 to 150 C)Higher, needs precise control
Strength / punctureHigherLower, more brittle
RecyclingRecyclable (LDPE stream)Limited; chlorine complicates it
Environmental profileCleaner, lower-carbonReleases HCl when burned; dioxin risk
Plasticizer riskNonePlasticizer migration possible
CostModerateLowest

3. Why PE Is Replacing PVC

PVC contains chlorine and can release corrosive hydrochloric acid and dioxins if incinerated; it is harder to recycle and faces tightening restrictions in several markets (notably EU packaging and waste rules, plus broader brand sustainability commitments). PE shrinks at lower temperature — better for heat-sensitive PET bottles — is tougher in transit, and fits common LDPE recycling streams. For most consumer-facing bundles today, PE is the safer long-term choice.

4. Thickness and Gauge

Shrink film is specified in microns. Typical ranges:

UseTypical thickness
Light retail multipacks25 to 40 um
Beverage / brewery bottles40 to 60 um
Cans / water bundles45 to 60 um
Heavy / industrial bundles80 to 100 um

5. Which Should You Choose

  • If you bundle beverages, beer, canned goods or retail multipacks and care about recyclability and brand image -> choose PE.
  • Match the film to your shrink tunnel temperature range; PE runs cooler than PVC.
  • Reserve PVC only for legacy equipment where retrofit cost is prohibitive — and plan the switch.

6. Cost vs Total Value

PE is not always the cheapest film per kilogram, but total cost includes waste, breakage and brand risk. A recyclable, compliant film avoids future retrofit and recall exposure, and supports recyclability claims on-pack. For most brands, PE is the better long-term value even where PVC is a few cents cheaper per roll.

FAQ

Is PVC banned?

Not everywhere, but it is increasingly restricted and is being phased out by brands for sustainability and compliance reasons.

Can I run PE on an existing PVC tunnel?

Usually yes — lower the tunnel temperature, because PE shrinks at a lower setpoint than PVC.

Which looks clearer?

PVC has marginally higher clarity, but modern PE is close enough for most retail packs.

Is PE recyclable?

Yes, PE shrink film typically enters the LDPE recycling stream, unlike PVC which is rarely curbside-recyclable.
