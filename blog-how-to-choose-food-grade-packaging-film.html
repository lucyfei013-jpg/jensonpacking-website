How to Choose Food Grade Packaging Film

Spec the right food-grade flexible film: regulations, material structures, thickness and barrier requirements for snacks, frozen and fresh food.

Specifying food-grade flexible film is less about price per kilogram and more about matching material, thickness and barrier to the product, the filling line and the shelf life you need. This guide walks through the decisions buyers actually face.

1. Confirm Food-Contact Compliance First

Before comparing films, confirm the material is legal for food contact in your target market. In the United States, food-contact plastics are governed by FDA 21 CFR 170 to 190; in the European Union by Framework Regulation (EC) 1935/2004 and Plastics Regulation (EU) 10/2011, with an overall migration limit of 10 mg/dm2; in China by the GB 4806 series (GB 4806.1 general plus GB 4806.7 for plastics). A film that lacks a valid compliance declaration cannot legally touch food, regardless of how cheap it is. Always request a written food-contact statement and, for printed film, confirmation that inks and adhesives are also compliant (typically low-migration, nestable to the food side).

2. Pick the Right Material Structure

Most food films are multi-layer laminates that combine a sealant layer, a strength layer and sometimes a barrier layer. Match the structure to what you are protecting:

StructureBest forWhy
LDPE / LLDPE (single layer)Frozen food, bakery, fresh produceLow cost, easy heat-seal, good clarity, food-safe
BOPP / CPPBiscuits, snacks, confectioneryCrisp, glossy, good moisture barrier, printable
PET / PEPowders, dried food, stand-up pouchesHigh strength, excellent print surface
PA (Nylon) / PEVacuum packs, cheese, sharp-edged itemsPuncture resistance, thermoformable, drop-proof
PET / ALU / PE or PET / MET-ALU / PECoffee, retort, long shelf lifeMaximum oxygen and moisture barrier
PET / EVOH / PEOxygen-sensitive dry foodTransparent high-barrier via EVOH layer

3. Match Thickness to the Product

Thickness is usually specified in microns (um). As a rule of thumb:

ApplicationTypical thicknessNote
Bakery / bread30 to 50 umSoft seal, moderate barrier
Snacks / biscuits20 to 40 umCrisp, moisture-protective
Frozen / ice cream60 to 100 umSurvives cold and handling
Vacuum meat / cheese70 to 120 um (PA/PE)Puncture resistance is key
Powder / spice sachet50 to 90 umSeal integrity against fine particles

Over-specifying wastes material and money; under-specifying causes leakers and returns. A short call with your supplier about fill weight, drop height and required shelf life usually settles the right gauge.

4. Do Not Ignore Barrier, Printing and Finish

For oxygen- or moisture-sensitive food, state your target shelf life up front so the structure is engineered for it. EVOH or aluminium-foil layers give oxygen barrier; metallized (AlOx) or foil gives both. If the pack is also your shelf presence, choose rotogravure for long runs or digital printing for short runs, with a matte, gloss or registered-matte finish.

5. Common Mistakes Buyers Make

  • Chasing the lowest price per kg and ignoring migration compliance.
  • Using one "general" film for every product — frozen and powder have opposite needs.
  • Forgetting seal-integrity testing on the filling line (seal strength plus dye-leak).
  • Specifying barrier the product does not need, adding avoidable cost.

FAQ

Is polyethylene film safe for food?

Yes — provided it is made from food-grade resin and supplied with a valid food-contact compliance declaration (FDA, EU 10/2011 or GB 4806 depending on market).

Can one film work for every product?

Rarely. Frozen, vacuum, powder and snack applications need different structures and thicknesses; using the wrong one risks leaks or spoilage.

Which certificates should I request from a supplier?

A food-contact compliance declaration for the target market, plus ISO 9001 as a baseline; BRCGS or similar is a plus for export.

How do I confirm the seal will hold?

Run seal-strength and dye-leak (or bubble) tests on your actual filling line with the specified structure and thickness.
